The Four-Square Prioritized Differential (FSPD) is a method for prioritizing a differential diagnosis along two axes — **likelihood** and **degree of harm if not recognized** — so that "must not miss" diagnoses are handled explicitly and early, and not buried in a long list. The point of the FSPD is to make explicit what is **probable** rather than what is possible.

The degree of overlap between a working diagnosis (in the form of a problem representation) and the **classic** presentation of a disease: this defines probability of the patient at hand having this diagnosis. Draw a 2 x 2 square and label the boxes as Type I, Ib, II, and III: 

|                       | Likely                       | Unlikely                        |
| --------------------- | ---------------------------- | ------------------------------- |
| **Highest probability**         | I                 | Ib   |
| **Lower probability**     | II  | III                           |

Never forget to consider 1b diagnoses: imminent threats to life if not recognized or acted upon within minutes to hours.
